# USER INTERVIEWS

# Goal

The purpose of these conversations was to better understand how people currently manage AI tooling subscriptions, where inefficiencies exist, and whether optimization recommendations would feel valuable in practice.

The interviews were informal exploratory conversations rather than structured enterprise research sessions.

---

# Interview 1 — Student Developer

## Profile

- MCA student
- Uses multiple AI coding tools
- Frequently experiments with new AI subscriptions

## Key Insights

The user mentioned frequently switching between:
- ChatGPT,
- Cursor,
- GitHub Copilot,
- and Gemini.

The biggest issue was:
- unclear overlap between tools,
- paying for tools that were not actively used,
- and difficulty deciding which subscriptions actually improved productivity.

## Important Quote

> “I keep paying for tools because I think I might need them later.”

## Product Insight

This reinforced the idea that AI tooling creates “subscription sprawl,” especially for technical users experimenting rapidly.

---

# Interview 2 — Startup-Oriented Developer

## Profile

- Works on side projects
- Uses AI tools for coding, writing, and research
- Small team collaboration environment

## Key Insights

The user mentioned:
- multiple team subscriptions,
- duplicated tooling,
- and confusion around whether enterprise/team plans were actually necessary.

They also mentioned that pricing pages for AI tools are often inconsistent and difficult to compare directly.

## Important Quote

> “We added team plans before we even knew if we needed them.”

## Product Insight

This validated the “right-sizing” recommendation logic in the audit engine.

---

# Interview 3 — Operations / Productivity-Focused User

## Profile

- Interested in SaaS productivity tools
- Uses AI mostly for writing and workflow automation

## Key Insights

The user cared less about advanced AI capabilities and more about:
- cost visibility,
- centralized reporting,
- and understanding recurring monthly spend.

The user preferred:
- clear summaries,
- simple dashboards,
- and actionable recommendations.

## Important Quote

> “I don’t want another analytics platform. I just want to know where I’m wasting money.”

## Product Insight

This reinforced the importance of:
- simplicity,
- concise reporting,
- and actionable outputs.

---

# Common Patterns Across Interviews

Several repeated themes appeared consistently:

## 1. AI Subscription Sprawl

Users frequently accumulate:
- overlapping subscriptions,
- duplicate capabilities,
- and unused plans.

---

## 2. Poor Cost Visibility

Most users do not actively track:
- AI subscription costs,
- annual spend,
- or ROI by tool.

---

## 3. Confusion Around Team Plans

Many users adopt:
- Team,
- Business,
- or Enterprise plans

before truly needing them.

---

## 4. Interest in Optimization Guidance

Users generally responded positively to:
- savings recommendations,
- tooling comparisons,
- and workflow-specific suggestions.

---

# What Changed Because of These Conversations

These conversations influenced several product decisions:

- emphasis on explainable recommendations,
- simplified dashboard presentation,
- savings-focused summaries,
- and public shareable reports.

The interviews also reinforced the decision to use deterministic rule-based logic rather than opaque AI-generated recommendations.

---

# Biggest Learning

The biggest insight was that AI tooling is creating a completely new category of operational confusion.

Users are adopting AI products extremely quickly, but very few people have systematic processes for:
- evaluating overlap,
- measuring ROI,
- or optimizing recurring AI spend.

This creates a strong opportunity for lightweight optimization tooling like SpendPilot AI.
